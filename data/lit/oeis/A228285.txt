            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%U 34,55,277,3154,10414,21166,21166,10414,3154,277,55,89,595,9637,44024,126119
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%N T(n,k) = Number of n X k binary arrays with top left value 1 and no two ones adjacent horizontally, vertically or nw-se diagonally.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%H R. H. Hardin, &lt;a href=&#34;/A228285/b228285.txt&#34;&gt;Table of n, a(n) for n = 1..1300&lt;/a&gt;
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%H Doron Zeilberger, &lt;a href=&#34;/A228285/a228285.txt&#34;&gt;Maple code for columns of A228285 and A226444&lt;/a&gt;
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%H Doron Zeilberger, &lt;a href=&#34;/A228285/a228285_2.txt&#34;&gt;Maple output for columns of A228285&lt;/a&gt;
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F Empirical for column k:
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=1: a(n) = a(n-1) &#43; a(n-2).
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=2: a(n) = a(n-1) &#43; 2*a(n-2) &#43; a(n-3).
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=3: a(n) = a(n-1) &#43; 5*a(n-2) &#43; 4*a(n-3) - a(n-5) with g.f. x(2&#43;x-x^3)/((1&#43;x)(1-2x-3x^2-x^3&#43;x^4)).
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=4: a(n) = a(n-1) &#43;10*a(n-2) &#43;15*a(n-3) &#43;4*a(n-4) -6*a(n-5) -a(n-6) &#43;3*a(n-7) -a(n-8) with g.f. x *(x-1) *(2*x^3 -5*x^2 -6*x -3) / ( 1 -x -10*x^2 -15*x^3 -4*x^4 &#43;6*x^5 &#43;x^6 -3*x^7 &#43;x^8 ).
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=5: [order 13] - see A228280.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=6: [order 21]
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=7: [order 34]
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=8: [order 55]
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F k=9: [order 89]
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F From _Doron Zeilberger_, Aug 19 2013 and Aug 22 2013: (Start)
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F Using the C-finite Ansatz one can show that the k-th column satisfies a recurrence of order F_{k&#43;2} for all k. For k &lt;= 11, this is the minimal order. The empirical g.f.&#39;s given above are correct. For further g.f.&#39;s and Maple code, see the links.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F In more detail: Every k X n Hardin matrix can be viewed as a walk, of length n, on a graph with F_{k&#43;2} vertices (labeled by the set of {0,1} vectors of length k that avoid two consecutive 1&#39;s, which is well known and fairly easy to see has cardinality F_{k&#43;2}).
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F Then the computer constructs the adjacency matrix.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F There is an edge between vertex v1 and vertex v2 only if it is NOT the case that there exists an i (1 &lt;= i &lt;= k) such that v1[i]=1 and v2[i]=1 AND it is not the case that there exists an i (1 &lt;= i &lt;= k-1) such that v1[i]=1 and v2[i&#43;1]=1.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F Let us call this matrix A(k).
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F Then the number of k X n Hardin matrices (without the restriction that the top-left entry is 1, A226444) is the sum of the entries (i,j) of A(k)^n, or equivalently (1,...., 1) A(k)^n (1, ...., 1)^T.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F So
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F f_k(x) = Sum_{n&gt;=0} a(n)*x^n
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F = (1,...., 1) Sum_{n&gt;=0} A(k)^n*x^n (1, ...., 1)^T
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F = (1,...., 1) (I-A*x)^(-1)(1, ...., 1)^T
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F and Maple knows how to invert the symbolic matrix (I-A*x), and this explains why the characteristic polynomial is the symbol for the recurrence.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F If we impose that restriction then the answer (A228285) is
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F [0-1-Vector with 1&#39;s for those whose first entry is 1] A(k)^n (1, ...., 1)^T.
</tt></p>
            
            <p style="text-indent: -2em; margin-left: 2em; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0;"><tt>%F (End)
</tt></p>
